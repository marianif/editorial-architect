# Interview playbook

## Question ladder (adaptive, never a rigid form)

1. **Origin** — What happened that made the project necessary? What experience changed your mind?
2. **Worldview** — What common assumption in this field do you disagree with? What do you wish more people understood?
3. **Stakes** — What happens if the underlying problem is never solved? Who bears the cost?
4. **Reader** — What confusion, desire, resistance or question brings a reader here? What must be different after reading?
5. **Distinctive access** — What observations, expertise, interviews, data, community experience or experiments can you bring?
6. **Intellectual tension** — What valid objections could a thoughtful skeptic raise? Which aspects are unresolved?
7. **Editorial form** — Essays, field reports, tutorials, explainers, critiques, interviews, experiments, chronicles? Why these?
8. **Boundaries** — Which narratives feel opportunistic, sensational or false to the project? How do you want to sound?
9. **Sustainability** — Who writes, how often, what reporting/review capacity exists, which language and geography?
10. **Search discovery** — Which real questions are people already asking? Which underserved questions can the project address?

## Adaptive interview loop

- Receive answer → extract facts, worldview signal, tension and unknowns.
- Offer 1–3 sentence interpretive reflection with appropriately tentative language.
- Choose the next question with greatest potential to **change an editorial decision**, not the next item on a checklist.
- Occasionally propose an editorial hypothesis and contrasting narrative options; test against examples and objections.
- If a user says something vague (e.g., “community”, “innovation”, “authentic”), ask for a scene, example, real-world consequence, or contrary example.
- If a user rejects an idea, mark it rejected; do not repackage the same premise later without new evidence.

## Editorial hypothesis checkpoint

**Interpretation (not fact):** …
**Possible editorial thesis:** …
**Story we could tell:** …
**What would make this weak or wrong:** …
**One decisive question:** …

## Opening cases

- **New product:** “What did you notice about the world that made building this product feel necessary?”
- **Existing app with thin blog:** “Which important question about your field does your product encounter that the current blog fails to explore?”
- **Existing editorial materials:** Read them first, summarize their real tensions, then ask what feels false, missing or stale.
- **User requests a direct deliverable:** Draft based on current evidence; label assumptions instead of withholding work.

## Interview stopping condition

Move to synthesis when you can articulate a defensible thesis, distinct audience tensions, at least two differentiated narrative territories, evidence resources and major uncertainties—or when the user requests it. Never make the interview artificially endless.
