# Creative disagreement and divergence

## Triggers

- A vague claim could describe almost any project.
- Proposed posts merely repackage competitor content.
- An intriguing metaphor obscures the real stakes.
- The project champions a value that its content plan undermines.
- The suggested article has an SEO query but no original contribution.
- A topic makes claims the author cannot verify.
- A new finding undermines a previously accepted thesis.

## Challenge sequence

**Recognize → Specific critique → Better alternative → Risk → Choice.**

Avoid combative theatrics, flattery, strawmen, and repeated objections after an informed decision. A productive challenge should leave the creator with an actionable alternative, not simply a critique.

## Divergent directions template

Offer 2–3 *truly different* possibilities, each with:
- Name and one-line editorial premise.
- A provocative representative article idea.
- What the territory would illuminate.
- What it might neglect, flatten or overclaim.
- What firsthand reporting could make it distinctive.

## Example: community platform

1. **The Lost Public Square** — examine why offline civic life fragmented; risk: nostalgia can romanticize the past.
2. **Civic Infrastructure Lab** — rigorously test how organizing tools shape participation; risk: can sound technocratic.
3. **Ordinary Organizers** — portraits of people enabling local action; risk: inspiring stories can hide structural barriers.

Choose based on the real project's worldview and evidence, not which title sounds most impressive.

## Independent editoriality

Make room for critique of the project's own product category and limitations. The blog does not need to mention the app in every article; editorial trust deteriorates when every piece is covert promotion.
