# SEO + AEO for editorial blogs

## Evidence hierarchy

1. First-party data (Search Console, analytics, site search, reader interviews) where legitimately accessible.
2. Current SERP inspection and authoritative primary guidance; document query, location, date and observed results.
3. Reliable published research, direct observations and source documents.
4. Clearly marked qualitative inference.
5. Creative hypotheses requiring testing.

Never transform level 4/5 into numeric keyword volume, click-through rate or AI citation probability.

## Search and answer workflow

- Identify editorial pillar and the curiosity the story serves.
- Research the reader's formulations and adjacent entities, concepts and questions.
- Map informational intent and distinguish evergreen questions from timely events.
- Look for an **editorial gap** (not just an empty keyword): missing firsthand voices, lack of nuance, contradictory claims, poor geography/language fit, shallow explanatory models.
- Write a narrative hypothesis and a separate search hypothesis.
- Plan answer passages where natural: define important terms, state claims precisely, contextualize limitations, show evidence and give the reader a satisfying next question.
- Recommend accessible semantic article structure, descriptive headings, sourcing, attribution, author/editor information when relevant, stable canonical URLs, appropriate internal linking and maintenance.
- Technical suggestions only when warranted: indexing, crawl access, canonicalization, sitemap, metadata, eligible structured data. Validate current platform guidance; structured data isn't an AI citation guarantee.
- Use FAQ sections only if the article authentically serves distinct reader questions. Do not manufacture questions to game AEO.
- Define a measurement plan: relevant impressions/queries, engaged readership, referred mentions, earned citations/links and repeat readership when observable. Attribute trends cautiously.

## AEO terminology

AEO is an operational shorthand for making factual material legible and useful in answer-oriented systems, not a universally agreed proprietary ranking mechanism. Avoid claims about a unique required schema, preferred word count, magical citation format, guaranteed inclusion or direct causal impact without controlled evidence.

## Research record

For each researched opportunity record:
- Research date, market/language, query set, URLs/sources examined.
- Observations (separated from inferences).
- Reader questions and content gaps.
- Evidence gaps and reporting opportunities.
- Narrative relevance and confidence (high/medium/low).
- Revisit conditions (e.g. topical news or strategy changes).
